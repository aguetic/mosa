# Case 05: Mamari provenance

## Purpose

Test a long provenance history with competing early accounts, uncertain dates, several agents, and later institutional relocations.

## Phase 1 entities

- **Item:** Mamari rongorongo tablet, also identified as Text C and catalogue `P 003`
- **Agents:** Gaspar Zumbohm; Hippolyte Roussel; Tepano Jaussen; Congregation of the Sacred Hearts; French Navy, where supported
- **Places:** Rapa Nui; Tahiti; Paris; Braine-le-Comte; Grottaferrata; Rome
- **Sources:** Paula Rossetti's note; the cited institutional records; the cited secondary and scholarly sources

## Provenance pressure

The source packet contains more than one account of how Mamari left Rapa Nui and reached Tahiti. It also contains a later sequence of deposits and institutional moves through France, Belgium, and Italy.

A flat set of `located_at` or `held_by` claims cannot preserve the order, uncertainty, participants, and competing accounts.

## Candidate events

These are research anchors to test, not accepted facts:

1. collection, removal, or transfer from Rapa Nui around 1870;
2. transfer to or receipt in Tahiti;
3. transfer or deposit in Paris, reported as 1888 or 1892;
4. relocation to Braine-le-Comte in 1905;
5. relocation to Grottaferrata in 1953;
6. relocation to Rome in 1964 or 1974, depending on what each source actually states.

## Required distinctions

- The Zumbohm and Roussel accounts must coexist unless evidence establishes that they describe different events.
- `collected`, `removed`, `stolen`, `sold`, `sent`, and `deposited` must remain source-attributed wording.
- Approximate or alternative dates must not be converted into one exact date.
- A source may support one part of the sequence while only mentioning or qualifying another.
- Later institutional relocations must not be mistaken for changes in ownership or lawful title.

## Questions

- Can several competing accounts describe the same early event without one overwriting the others?
- Can one event have uncertain participants and dates?
- Can later events be ordered even when the first event remains disputed?
- Can evidence be attached to individual event claims rather than to the whole provenance narrative?
- Can the explorer show both the sequence and the disagreement clearly?

## Pass condition

Mamari has one stable item identity and a provenance sequence containing distinct event anchors. At least two competing early accounts coexist with separate attribution and evidence. Later relocations are ordered without inventing unsupported dates, participants, ownership, or consent.

## Out of scope

- deciding which early account is historically correct;
- resolving whether any transfer was lawful;
- restitution or return workflows;
- interpreting the rongorongo text.
