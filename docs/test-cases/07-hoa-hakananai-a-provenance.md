# Case 07: Hoa Hakananaiʻa provenance

## Purpose

Test a documented naval removal that is described differently by institutional, community, Chilean, and secondary sources.

## Phase 1 entities

- **Item:** Hoa Hakananaiʻa
- **Agents:** HMS *Topaze* expedition; Richard Ashmore Powell; British Admiralty; Queen Victoria; British Museum; relevant Rapa Nui community authorities
- **Places:** Orongo; Rapa Nui; London
- **Sources:** British Museum catalogue record; Paula Rossetti's note; direct community or Chilean institutional sources when available; cited secondary sources

## Provenance pressure

The item's removal in 1868 is relatively well documented, but its meaning and legitimacy are contested. Sources may describe it as removed, collected, taken, stolen, presented, donated, or transferred.

The event model must preserve a shared historical anchor while allowing incompatible characterisations and incomplete evidence about authority or consent.

## Candidate events

1. removal from Orongo during the HMS *Topaze* expedition in 1868;
2. transport from Rapa Nui to Britain;
3. presentation or transfer involving the Admiralty and Queen Victoria;
4. transfer to or accession by the British Museum;
5. later custody and display changes, only where directly evidenced.

## Required distinctions

- The 1868 removal is an event; `stolen`, `collected`, and `removed` are competing characterisations of it.
- The ship, expedition, commander, Admiralty, monarch, museum, and Rapa Nui authorities have distinct participant roles.
- Presentation to Queen Victoria and accession by the British Museum must not be collapsed unless the evidence supports one event.
- Current custody must not imply ownership, lawful title, or legitimate removal.
- Community positions known only through Paula's note use indirect evidence until direct sources are captured.

## Questions

- Can one event carry several incompatible source-attributed descriptions?
- Can a documented date coexist with uncertainty about authority, consent, and participant roles?
- Can transport, presentation, accession, and custody remain separate events or relationships?
- Can direct and indirect community evidence be distinguished?
- Can the explorer show disagreement without presenting one wording as MoSA's conclusion?

## Pass condition

The 1868 removal is represented once as a stable event anchor with separately attributed characterisations and evidence. Subsequent transport, presentation, accession, and custody are not conflated. No claim implies ownership, consent, legality, or lawful title unless a source explicitly asserts it.

## Out of scope

- restitution requests, negotiations, or proposed exchanges;
- a legal determination that the removal was theft or lawful acquisition;
- sensitivity and publication controls;
- resolving the preferred public narrative.
