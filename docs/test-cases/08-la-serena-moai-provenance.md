# Case 08: La Serena moai provenance

## Purpose

Test how the provenance model handles a sparse transfer report without inventing actors, authority, dates, or legal conclusions.

## Phase 1 entities

- **Item:** Stone moai associated with the archaeological museum in La Serena
- **Agents:** Archaeological museum in La Serena; unidentified persons or groups involved in the reported transfer
- **Places:** Rapa Nui; La Serena
- **Sources:** Paula Rossetti's note and any later direct institutional or archival sources

## Provenance pressure

The current note states that the moai was taken in 1952 and that it is said to have been a gift from the Rapa Nui people.

This is enough to justify a provisional event anchor, but not enough to identify the giver, recipient, authority, route, exact date, or legal character of the transfer.

## Candidate event

1. a transfer or removal associated with the year 1952 and the object's later presence in La Serena.

## Required distinctions

- `1952` may identify only a year, not an exact date.
- `se dice que fue un regalo` is a reported characterisation, not an accepted event type.
- `the Rapa Nui people` must not be converted into a specific community agent without a source identifying one.
- The museum's later custody does not establish that it received the item directly in 1952.
- Unknown participants, route, place of transfer, consent, and authority must remain unknown.

## Questions

- Can an event be represented when most participants and details are unknown?
- Can a reported `gift` description remain attributed to the source rather than becoming canonical?
- Can year-level precision be preserved?
- Can later evidence extend the event without replacing or rewriting the original note?
- Can the explorer make missing information as visible as recorded information?

## Pass condition

A provisional 1952 event can be represented with year-level precision and evidence from Paula's note. The gift description remains indirect and attributed. No specific giver, recipient, authority, consent, route, ownership, or legal status is invented.

## Out of scope

- deciding whether the transfer was a gift;
- determining who had authority to give the item;
- reconstructing an unsupported route;
- restitution, legal review, or publication decisions.
